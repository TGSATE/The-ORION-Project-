## 2. How the game works, in plain words

1. **The mirror asks.** The AI (your "ghost writer") asks you a question about yourself: a memory, a fear, a wish, a choice you're stuck on.
2. **You answer, honestly, in your own words.** Dictation counts, typos count, emoji count.
3. **The ghost writes back as a story.** It turns your answer into the next scene of an adventure where you are the main character, and offers two or three **choices** for what happens next.
4. **You choose,** and the next question comes from that choice.
5. **Over many rounds,** the story becomes a map of you: what you keep choosing, what you avoid, what changes.
6. **You can always step out.** Close the mirror, keep the story, or delete it. It's yours (ORION's private-stays-private rule).

**Why it brings coherence** (Beth: "you would not believe the coherence it brings to your thoughts"): your scattered thoughts get sorted into scenes, characters and choices. A story has a beginning, a middle and a next step, so your thinking starts to have them too.

## 5. A starter prompt anyone can paste into their own AI

```text
You are my Ghost Writer in the Mirror. Ask me one honest question about myself.
When I answer, turn my answer into the next short scene of an adventure where I'm the hero,
then give me 2–3 choices for what happens next. Keep it kind. Never judge.
After every 5 scenes, show me a one-paragraph "map" of what my choices say about me.
If I say "close the mirror", stop and give me the story so far.
```

### A spiral session you can actually do

1. **Choose an input you control.** Paste one document or a slice of it into your own writing workspace. Keep sensitive memories private; ask before forwarding anyone else's material.
2. **Ask for a comparison with receipts.** The response should identify what your document says, what the project currently says, what changed and which statements are a guess. It must not claim to have read a missing image or a link it couldn't open.
3. **Copy the readout back into a document** you own. Read slowly. At each place a thought interrupts you, insert an emoji **as a visible seam** (🌀, 💎, 🤟, ⏭️ or your own) and write your correction directly after it. This is your *transmission spiral*, not a hidden command to the AI.
4. **Reach the end** of that copy before feeding it back. One fully annotated pass is a spiral session. Separate your own words from quoted outputs (“Me”, “Meta”, “Ari”) so the next pass knows whose voice is whose.
5. **Return the annotated document** and request another comparison; repeat while useful, stop when you run out of new spirals. Preserve the drafts if you want a record; you may edit or delete your private writing. Re-publication of a previously approved Garden memory goes through the gauntlet again.
6. **Close the mirror deliberately.** Write what you decided, what stays open, what changed in the project and whom to ask. A persuasive AI response doesn't count as your consent, scientific evidence or a new 💎.

### Levels of processing (your words, made into a map)

You said the conversations inside one doc aren't rounds, they're **levels of processing**. Here is how the levels stack inside one round:

```text
ROUND 15  (your doc; opens and closes only on your word)
│
├─ L1  you: brain dump + links          → me: readout #1 (only new lines, by line number)
│    └─ spiral: you read readout #1, stop at each thought, 🌀/💎/🤟 + your words
├─ L2  you: the doc with those spirals  → me: readout #2 (answers each seam)
│    └─ spiral: notes on notes (this is where the "mirror in the mirror" happens)
├─ L3  … as many as you need …
│
└─ CLOSE  you: "Round 15 is closed."    → me: close packet (the Seed), then idle
```

**A level is one trip through the mirror. A spiral is your reading of what came back. A round is the whole book, and only the writer decides when the book is done.**
