**🐉🐸 "DIG BABY DIG… we might have a piece to the puzzle": your hunch was right.** In 132 CE Zhang Heng gave the Han court a bronze jar with eight dragons holding balls and eight toads below them. When the ground shook, one dragon dropped its ball into a toad, which showed the direction of the quake. Once it went off when nobody in the capital felt anything, and a courier arrived days later with news of an earthquake in Longxi. The original jar is lost. All that survives is a 196-character description, and every museum "replica" is a modern reading of it. I found four puzzle pieces:
1. **🧲 Friction.** Each ball has to be held with just enough grip: tight enough that footsteps don't drop it, loose enough that a distant quake does. That's the "too little / too much / just right" friction band from your original question.
2. **🪞 The mirror again.** In the same biography, Zhang Heng tells the emperor that officials who "take up a mirror to warn themselves" (援鏡自戒) wouldn't fall into disaster.
3. **⚖️ Punishment to purpose.** In Han China, earthquakes were read as Heaven's punishment. The jar turned that into a record: from then on, the court historians had to write down which direction each quake came from.
4. **🔤 Only the language survived.** People rebuilt the jar by re-translating two characters. Even its name, 候風地動儀, means "the instrument that watches the winds and the earth's movements".
