Houfeng Didong Yi - The Ancient Chinese Earthquake Detector :-

In 132 CE, a Chinese scientist built a device that could detect earthquakes without anyone feeling the shaking nearby.

His name was Zhang Heng, and his invention was called the Houfeng Didong Yi.

Picture a bronze vessel surrounded by eight dragon heads, each holding a small ball in its mouth. Below them sat eight toads, ready to catch the balls.

When an earthquake triggered the device’s internal mechanism, one ball would drop into a toad’s mouth, indicating the direction of the tremor.

According to historical accounts, it once detected a distant earthquake that people in the capital hadn’t felt. Days later, news reportedly confirmed the event.

Think about that. Nearly 1,900 years ago, people were already trying to detect earthquakes from far away.

There’s one mystery, though: the original device no longer exists, and its exact mechanism remains uncertain.
