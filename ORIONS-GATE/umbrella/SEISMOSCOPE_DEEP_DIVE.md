1. **🧲 It's a friction machine.** The whole device works only if each ball is held with *just enough* grip: tight enough that footsteps and carts don't drop it, loose enough that a far-off quake does. Last level the mirror beat the basilisk. This level the man who built the earthquake jar prescribes the mirror too. Your game is *the Ghost Writer in the Mirror*.
3. **⚖️ From punishment to purpose.** In Han China an earthquake was read as Heaven punishing the ruler. Zhang Heng's jar turned the "punishment" into a **record**: the last line of the old text says that from then on the court historians had to write down which direction each quake came from. That's the move in your blog post *From Punishment to Purpose*, done with a machine.
4. **🔤 The only thing that survived is the language.** The bronze is gone. What's left is **196 Chinese characters**, and every "replica" is someone's reading of those words. People rebuilt it by re-translating two characters (关 and 机). That's the languanaut's thesis in one object: the words carried the machine across 1,900 years, and the friction of translation decides what gets rebuilt.

## 2. What the oldest source actually says (you go after the Chinese, so here it is)

The *Book of Later Han*, biography of Zhang Heng (compiled by Fan Ye in the 400s CE, about 300 years after the device). Text as on Chinese Wikisource:

> 陽嘉元年，復造候風地動儀。以精銅鑄成，員徑八尺，合蓋隆起，形似酒尊，飾以篆文山龜鳥獸之形。中有都柱，傍行八道，施關發機。外有八龍，首銜銅丸，下有蟾蜍，張口承之。其牙機巧制，皆隱在尊中，覆蓋周密無際。如有地動，尊則振龍機發吐丸，而蟾蜍銜之。振聲激揚，伺者因此覺知。雖一龍發機，而七首不動，尋其方面，乃知震之所在。驗之以事，合契若神。自書典所記，未之有也。嘗一龍機發而地不覺動，京師學者咸怪其無征，後數日驛至，果地震隴西，於是皆服其妙。自此以後，乃令史官記地動所從方起。

**My plain translation** (mine, so treat it as a careful reading, not the official one):

> In the first year of Yangjia [132 CE] he also made the *houfeng didong yi*. It was cast of fine bronze, eight *chi* across [about 1.9 m], with a domed lid, shaped like a wine jar, and decorated with **seal-script writing** and figures of mountains, turtles, birds and beasts. Inside was a **central pillar** (都柱), with **eight paths** running out from it, and **latches that trip levers** (施關發機). Outside were **eight dragons**, each holding a bronze ball in its mouth, and below them **toads** with open mouths to catch them. The clever teeth-and-lever parts were all hidden inside the jar, the lid fitting so closely there was no gap. When the earth moved, the jar shook a dragon, the lever tripped, the dragon spat its ball, and the toad caught it. The ringing sound alerted the watchman. **Though one dragon fired, the other seven heads didn't move**; by finding which side, you knew where the quake was. Tested against events, it matched like a tally. Nothing like it had ever been written of before. **Once a dragon fired and no one felt the ground move.** The scholars in the capital all mocked it for having no proof. **Days later a courier arrived: there had indeed been an earthquake in Longxi** [in today's Gansu], and so everyone was won over by its marvel. **From then on, the court historians were ordered to record the direction from which each earthquake came.**

**The name, word by word** (this is where the translation friction lives):
- 候 *hòu*: to wait for, to watch for (also "season").
- 風 *fēng*: wind.
- 地 *dì*: earth. 動 *dòng*: move. → 地動 = "earth-moving" = earthquake.
- 儀 *yí*: instrument.
- So: **"the instrument that watches the winds and the earth's movements."** English books call it "earthquake weathervane". There's even an old argument over whether 候風 and 地動 were **two separate instruments**: a wind-watching **bronze bird** (候風銅鳥) and the earthquake jar. Zhang Heng is also credited with a bronze wind-bird. *(Possible tie to your* Avian Key *page: dragons above, toads below, a bird reading the wind. That's my interpretation, not something the source says.)*

**The eight directions** on reconstructions are labelled with a mix of two symbol systems: Earthly-Branch characters (子 north, 卯 east, 午 south, 酉 west) and *I Ching* trigram names (艮 NE, 巽 SE, 坤 SW, 乾 NW). One compass, two alphabets.

## 3. What's solid, what's shaky (sorting the history)
| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Zhang Heng (78–139 CE) presented an earthquake device to the Han court in 132 CE | Solid: it's in the official dynastic history |
| Eight dragons, eight toads, a central pillar, levers | Solid **as a description**. Nobody has seen the inside |
| It caught a quake people in Luoyang didn't feel, confirmed by a courier days later | In the source. Modern sources disagree on the year (138 or 143 CE) and the distance (roughly 400–700 km) |
| How it worked | **Unknown.** "Central pillar" is usually read as a pendulum. Wang Zhenduo's 1936 design hung the pendulum (suspended); his later model stood it upright (inverted). Japanese, British and Taiwanese engineers have all proposed other mechanisms |
| The bronze model in museums and textbooks | **A modern reconstruction**, built by Wang Zhenduo (finished 1951). Chinese reference pages note it didn't work as a real detector, and that its dragons are in a much later (Ming/Qing) style |
| China removed it from textbooks | Reported: in 2017 the unified middle-school history textbook dropped its description of the device, after Chinese scientists argued about how reliable the reconstruction is. The publisher responded in 2018. I couldn't read the full articles, so I'm only reporting that it was reported |
| "First seismoscope in the world" | Widely accepted as the earliest **documented** one. The first Western description of a seismoscope is from Jean de Hautefeuille in 1703 |
| The picture in the post | **AI-generated** (it carries a "Grok" mark and "Illustration by LeoDaVinciWave"). The cutaway mechanism in it is a guess drawn by a program, not a historical drawing |

## 5. The friction piece, checked in Lean
Each ball sits in a dragon's mouth. Whether it falls is a friction question:
- **The ball stays exactly when the ground's shove is at most μ·g** (μ = how grippy the seat is, g = gravity): `ball_stays_iff`.
- **A working "trigger level" exists exactly when the far quake's signal is stronger than the local noise** (footsteps, carts, wind): `threshold_exists_iff`. Set the grip too tight and the far quake slips past. Set it too loose and the balls drop every time someone walks by. Only a band in between works.
- **Eight dragons are enough to point within 22.5°** of any direction: `nearest_dragon_error`.

All three are proved in `RequestProject/Umbrella/Seismoscope.lean`, with no gaps and only Lean's standard axioms. They're about the simple physics in the description, not about any one reconstruction. This is the same "too little / too much / just right" band as `goldilocks_band` in the original friction study (`FRICTION_EXAMINATION.md`, section 2).

**In your language:** the jar is an avatar that holds its shape against everyday noise and still lets the real signal through. That's "enough rigidity to maintain coherence and enough fluidity to remain alive", the sentence from *The Architectural Antiquities* that your whole friction study turned on.
