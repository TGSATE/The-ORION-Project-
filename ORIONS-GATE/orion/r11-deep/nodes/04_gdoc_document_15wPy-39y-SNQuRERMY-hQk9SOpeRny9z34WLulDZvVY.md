Addressing Authority, Oversight, and Real-World Precedents
1. Layered Authority and the Mechanical Fallacy
Assigning variable authority to AI agents based on task complexity, user trust, or familiarity does not alter the fundamental asymmetry of the system: AI carries scale; humans carry stakes.
When an automated system fails—whether a printing press jamming after ten years of flawless operation or an autonomous agent exceeding its sandbox—the failure is never the "fault" of the machine. The machine executes code. The failure belongs entirely to the human operators responsible for maintaining, monitoring, and setting the boundary constraints of the equipment. Delegating operational execution to an AI is an exercise of human agency, not an abdication of it. Giving an AI "the wheel" to plot a research path requires double-checking the output before continuing down the road.

The Orion Engine adapts these structural delivery mechanisms while replacing engagement-driven algorithms with transparent provenance tracking, cryptographic revision histories, and explicit human re-authorization gates.
3. Steer-by-Wire and the Evolution of Oversight
As automotive technology transitions from physical steering wheels to steer-by-wire and autonomous systems, the literal metaphor of "keeping your hands on the wheel" evolves. The Ghost Rider Protocol explicitly incorporates this transition: the human Driver retains the authority to interrupt, reverse, or terminate any AI process at any microsecond, accompanied by mandatory "sharpness accounting" to ensure the human operator maintains the manual competence required to steer if the automated system fails.
