ORION Social Media 
ROUND TWO SUMMARY
I. The Inverted Architecture & The Bedrock Role
* The Inverted Stack: The system has flipped the standard Web2 architectural model. The Social Center now acts as the Bedrock or "Ego" layer at the absolute bottom of the platform's stack.
* The Drainage Basin: While verified rigor and formal logic flow upward into the Global Project Platform, the social square acts as the drainage basin. All community friction, personal achievements, and ego drain downward into the unified public identity.
II. The "One You" Identity Architecture & Resumes
* Backend Verification (The Vault): The system enforces a strict one-account-per-human policy utilizing KYC and biometric verification. The engine backend knows your real-world legal name to permanently prevent burner accounts, bot swarms, and ban evasion.
* Frontend Privacy (The Shield): Your real-world government name is strictly hidden from the public to prevent doxxing and corporate prejudice. Users operate under a single, unified ORION Handle across all four platforms.
* Aesthetic Freedom vs. Record Permanence: Players can change their avatar skins, genders, or digital clothing at will across platforms. However, their Master Accountability Record—which tracks behavior, achievements, and friction—remains permanently anchored to their single handle.
* The Dual-Track Resume: The public portfolio differentiates organic gamers from formal academics visually at a glance.
   * Black Dot to Color Spectrum: Passive, organic gameplay inside the MMO fills skill meters through a visual color spectrum, representing raw Field Knowledge. Players can build massive Field Knowledge resumes without formal schooling.
   * The Glowing Rainbow Crystal Cap: The terminal node on the skill meter. It is physically impossible to acquire through gameplay alone; unlocking it requires official enrollment in the Education Hub, passing the Council Gauntlet, and completing verified out-of-platform real-world study.
* Opt-In Resumes: While the system tracks all this data automatically via immutable receipts, players maintain control over curating it into a formal resume when they are ready to present themselves to the Global Project Platform or real-world recruiters.
III. The "Zoo" Epistemic Social Culture
* Dismantling Echo Chambers: The Monolithic Zoo shifts the social culture away from "like/agree" metrics and toward survival under rigorous criticism, dismantling Kronos-state echo chambers.
* Public "Zoo Challenge" Threads: Users post claims to the public feed, and the community actively invokes specific animal operators (such as the Tortoise, Chimera, Dragon, Phoenix, or Owl) against them to test their validity.
* Named-Animal Reputation Badges: Instead of generic popularity scores, profiles display specific adversarial survival metrics (e.g., "This user survived 12 Tortoise attacks exposing circularity").
* Anti-Herding Culture: The Zoo’s explicit goal is survivability under criticism, deliberately preventing a culture of consensus theater or simple agreement.
IV. Formal Verification, Provenance & Anti-Patterns
* The Grammar of Truth Loop: Social claims can utilize the formal verification loop (\text{Play} = \text{Seed} \rightarrow \text{Run(Rules)} \rightarrow \text{Certificate} \rightarrow \text{Datagram} \rightarrow \text{Anchor}).
* Public Anchor + Datagram Patterns: The system allows for posting short hashes (Public Anchors) in plain text that point to full, canonical claim packets. This ensures content survives platform rewriting of links and maintains verifiable provenance.
* Client-Side Dual Checker (Verify This Claim): The Social Center can embed a "Verify this claim" button that runs a dual-lens check client-side within the browser, returning a short hash without requiring server trust.
* Soft-Hide + Receipt Continuity: The platform retains the "No Silencing" soft-hide rule, but adds cryptographic continuity. When a comment is soft-hidden, the SHA-256 receipt chain still records the original content, the public reason, and the hash tip, providing continuity without turning the feed into an immutable blockchain nightmare.
* Anti-Pattern "Agreement Reward": The system explicitly bans the "Agreement Reward" (giving users points simply for agreeing with the crowd) as a direct warning against herding in review systems and social feeds.
* Named Reviewers vs. Echo Chambers: Instead of rewarding agreement, "Named Reviewers" earn prestige by successfully finding logic flaws through playable tools, and this prestige can appear as profile badges or comment metadata.
V. Intent, Archiving, and Lore
* The "Build" Conversation Toggle: To set intent, chat sessions feature a "Build" toggle. Toggling a chat to "Build" visually signals to the team that the brainstorming session is intended for potential project inclusion, allowing members to instantly shift mental gears from casual "Private" banter to focused mechanics.
* The Cross-Platform Archivist Role: Game archives are an Overall Platform Project Archive. An "Archivist" sitting directly on the Social Media Platform can browse, pick up, and contribute to an archived MMO build or real-world project blueprint without ever needing to log into the game engine.
* Epistemically Tagged Quote Bank: Famous cross-tradition quotes (e.g., Campbell, Yoda, Dune, Tesla) are mapped to the Hero's Journey and tagged with A-D vault rules to populate public interactions and guide posts.
VI. Pacing, Spam & Safety Protocols
* The Nuclear Spam Protocol: Sending more than one message per 30 seconds after five minutes of continuous chat triggers an immediate 1-minute global cooldown. A third infraction triggers a hard lock of 1 message per minute until cleared by a council. Ignoring this lock for 10 minutes initiates a Public Blackout, permanently restricting the account to 1-on-1 and group chats until appealed.
* Macro Buffers: The AI council actively monitors for macros skirting the 1-second buffer zone. Public feeds and skyboxes cannot alter the spam filter, though private Guild Halls can.
* The Black Hole Feast Access: During the monthly 24-hour global MMO shutdown—where the cosmos exponentially feeds off itself to rebalance—public platforms (including the Social Center) remain online for live skill updates, social interaction, and portfolio curation.
* Under-18 Guardianship & Locking: Under-18 accounts require strict legal guardian consent. Parent accounts receive live updates on progression and concerns; if the parent ignores system prompts, the child’s account is hard-locked.
