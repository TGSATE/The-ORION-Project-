Expansion asks whether bounded-length moves push a uniformly significant amount of mass out of every sufficiently small set. The basic ingredients Let G be a groupoid with unit space G⁽⁰⁾ , equipped with a probability measure μ and a length function ℓ. The length vanishes on units, is unchanged by inversion, and is subadditive under composition. A bisection is a subset on which both source and range are injective; it therefore acts as a measurable partial bijection of the unit space. Admissible bisections have measurable source and range, finite length, and preserve the measure class. Their Radon–Nikodym derivatives record how the measure changes under the induced partial bijection. A finite union of admissible bisections is N-decomposable ; symmetry means the pieces occur together with their inverses. Expansion compares the new boundary μ(r(K · A) \ A) with the mass μ(A), using moves K of uniformly bounded length. Expansion in measure A groupoid is (C, N, L)-expanding in measure when there is a unital, symmetric, N-decomposable set K of length at most L such that every measurable A with 0 < μ(A) ≤ 1/2 satisfies μ(r(K · A) \ A) > C μ(A). Asymptotic expansion weakens the uniformity requirement: for each lower-mass threshold α > 0, one may choose new constants C α , N α , L α that work for all sets with α ≤ μ(A) ≤ 1/2. This distinction allows tiny exceptional regions while retaining expansion on sets of controlled mass. Domains and Markov expansion A measurable subset Y of the unit space is a domain of asymptotic expansion when the reduced groupoid on Y has that property. One can strengthen the setup by requiring the Radon–Nikodym derivatives of the bisection pieces to be bounded above and below by a ratio ρ. This bounded-ratio condition makes it possible to normalize the local moves into a Markov kernel. Given bisections K i , the kernel at x assigns weights proportional to the square roots of their Radon–Nikodym derivatives, among the pieces that remain inside Y. The measure with density equal to the total weight is reversing for this kernel. Its Cheeger constant measures the least normalized flow crossing from a set to its complement. Structural viewpoint. The synthesis describes an equivalence between global asymptotic expansion, measured exhaustions by bounded-ratio expansion domains, and exhaustions by Markov-expanding domains with a uniform positive Cheeger bound. The passage uses maximal Følner sets, derivative truncation, and the Cheeger inequality. Finite propagation, quasi-locality, and ghosts An operator has finite dynamical propagation if it has no interaction between measurable regions separated by some bounded family of groupoid moves. A dynamically quasi-local operator has arbitrarily small interaction between such regions once the allowed propagation scale is chosen appropriately. The norm closure of finite-propagation operators gives the dynamical Roe algebra; the quasi-local operators form its larger comparison algebra. For a rank-one projection P onto a unit vector ξ, define its associated probability measure by dν = |ξ|² dμ. The stated characterization identifies membership of P in the dynamical Roe algebra, membership in the dynamical quasi-local algebra, and asymptotic expansion of the groupoid in ν. For a disjoint union of groupoids, the corresponding direct-sum criterion requires uniform asymptotic expansion across the components.
