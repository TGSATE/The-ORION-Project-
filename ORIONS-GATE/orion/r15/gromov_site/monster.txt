Graphical small cancellation Begin with a labelled graph Γ. Each oriented edge receives a generator or its inverse, and labels around simple cycles become relators. The quotient of a base group by these relators is the graphical presentation group. A piece is a word that occurs along two distinct embedded paths in the labelled graph. Graphical Gr′(λ) conditions constrain piece lengths relative to cycle lengths. When λ is sufficiently small (the classical threshold is below 1/6), small-cancellation geometry supplies strong control over the quotient, including hyperbolicity and—in the setting summarized here—torsion-freeness and infinitude. Choosing the expander graphs The input sequence Γ n must combine expansion with geometric control. The summary imposes unbounded graph sizes, diameter bounded by a constant times girth, and a thinness condition controlling how many short simple paths there are. These constraints make it possible to choose random labels while managing the chance that some path label becomes unexpectedly short in the base group. Subdividing each edge by a factor j scales girth and diameter by j and reduces the effective thinness exponent. It preserves the underlying expansion behavior up to the corresponding subdivision scale, while creating extra room for the probabilistic estimates. The inductive construction Start Choose an infinite torsion-free hyperbolic base group with spectral radius below 1. Label Randomly label a carefully chosen expander graph and control short path words. Quotient Impose the cycle relators using graphical small cancellation. Iterate Repeat, retaining uniform spectral control and embeddings at larger scales. At each stage, probabilistic estimates and hyperbolicity promote local control of path labels to global quasi-geodesic behavior. The natural map from Γ n into the next group is quantitatively quasi-isometric over the graph’s relevant scale. A uniform spectral-radius bound—associated in the supplied construction with property (T) at an early stage—helps keep the estimates from deteriorating through the induction. G 0 → G 1 → G 2 → ··· ; G ∞ = lim → G n . Why coarse Hilbert embedding fails Expander graphs have large distortion in Hilbert space: across each graph there are pairs that are far apart in the graph metric but cannot all be sent far apart in Hilbert space by a uniformly controlled map. If the graph metrics are preserved up to controlled distortion when the expanders enter G ∞ , these pairs remain a problem in the limit group. More concretely, the lower quasi-isometric estimate for the embedding of Γ n into G ∞ turns graph distances on the order of girth into distances tending to infinity in the group. A coarse embedding with a 1-Lipschitz upper control would have to send the expander witness pairs arbitrarily far apart; the expander Hilbert-distortion estimate supplies pairs whose images stay bounded. The two conclusions conflict. Conclusion in this framework. The direct-limit group does not coarsely embed into a Hilbert space. The obstruction is carried by the expander family embedded into its large-scale geometry. How this relates to assembly theory The monster-group argument and the ℓ p box-space obstruction use expanders differently. In the former, expanders are geometrically embedded into a group to obstruct coarse Hilbert embedding. In the latter, finite quotients form an expander box space and a Kazhdan idempotent obstructs assembly surjectivity. Both rely on spectral gaps, but neither conclusion should be substituted for the other.
