On expander box spaces, a Kazhdan idempotent can expose a mismatch between global K-theory and local geometric data. Roe and localization algebras For a bounded-geometry metric space X, the algebraic ℓ p Roe algebra is built from locally compact operators of finite propagation on a suitable ℓ p module. Its norm closure is denoted B p (X). Finite propagation means matrix entries vanish whenever the corresponding points are farther apart than a fixed scale. The localization algebra B p L (X) consists, schematically, of bounded uniformly continuous operator families indexed by t ≥ 0, with propagation tending to zero as t tends to infinity. Evaluation at t = 0, combined with Rips complexes at increasing scales, defines the coarse assembly map μ : lim R→∞ K * (B p L (P R (X))) → K * (B p (X)). This is a Banach-algebraic analogue of the familiar C * -algebraic construction. Equivariant versions can often be compared with reduced ℓ p group algebras when a group acts freely and properly, while dual ℓ p structures and complex interpolation connect the analysis to the Hilbert-space setting. Why localization matters Assembly is designed to build global operator K-classes from increasingly local geometric information. For groups with suitable analytic properties—such as the hyperbolic-group setting described in the supplied material—injectivity can be obtained by comparing the ℓ p theory to classical Baum–Connes methods. A covering-space induction isomorphism can also transport localization classes between a quotient and its regular cover. Key distinction. Injectivity and surjectivity are separate questions. The expander example targets surjectivity: it constructs a nonzero Roe-algebra K-class that cannot come from localization data. Operator norm localization The p-operator norm localization property (p-ONLP) says, roughly, that a finite-propagation operator has a near-norm-attaining vector supported in a uniformly bounded region. The support bound depends on the propagation scale, not on the full size of the space. Metric sparsification supplies this property in many settings, including the hyperbolic-group framework in the supplied synthesis. For a box space □G = ⨆ G/N i , finite-propagation operators on sufficiently large quotient components can be lifted to operators on G that are invariant under N i . The resulting map into a product modulo a direct sum retains the tail behavior of those lifts. The localization property is then used to show that ghost operators vanish under this lifting map. The expander obstruction Assume G has property τ relative to a family of finite-index subgroups. The associated Kazhdan idempotent q = ⊕q i projects onto invariant vectors on each finite quotient. In the box-space Roe algebra it is a non-compact ghost: its matrix entries fade at large distances, although its K-class remains detectable. 1 · Trace The componentwise trace detects the constant class [1, 1, …], so [q] is nonzero. 2 · Lift p-ONLP forces the ghost class to vanish under the tail lifting map. 3 · Injectivity Assembly injectivity on the covering groups eliminates sufficiently far-out localized classes. 4 · Contradiction The trace of an assembled class would vanish in the tail, unlike the trace of [q]. Under the hypotheses in the cited theorem, this contradiction shows that the ℓ p coarse assembly map is not surjective for the expander box space. The argument combines a nonzero K-class, ghost vanishing after lifting, and injectivity for the relevant groups. Pseudolocal operators and obstruction groups Pseudolocal operators almost commute with functions vanishing at infinity: their commutators with C 0 (X) are compact. Localization versions of these algebras provide an alternative model for the assembly map. In the framework summarized here, the localization pseudolocal algebra has trivial K-theory, yielding a boundary-map description of Roe localization K-theory. Related obstruction groups can be formulated using localization kernels or pseudolocal quotients on Rips complexes. Their non-vanishing gives equivalent criteria for failure of the assembly map to be an isomorphism, subject to the framework’s hypotheses. The shared theme with groupoid expansion is that expansion creates a ghost-like operator. Here the obstruction is read through K-theory and assembly rather than only through membership in a Roe algebra.
