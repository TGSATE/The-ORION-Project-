### Tonatiuh: The Fifth Sun (Turquoise Lord)
For the Aztecs, Tonatiuh was not just the sun in the sky; he was the active, reigning force of the current era, which they called the "Fifth Sun" [citation:2][citation:6].

*   **A Universe Born of Sacrifice:** The Aztecs believed four previous worlds had been created and destroyed. To create the fifth and current sun, the gods had to sacrifice themselves at the ancient city of Teotihuacan. A humble, afflicted god named Nanahuatzin bravely threw himself into a fire, emerging as Tonatiuh, the new sun [citation:2].
*   **A Constant Struggle:** Once born, Tonatiuh refused to move. The other gods knew they had to sacrifice themselves to give him the energy to begin his journey across the sky. This established the core Aztec belief: **the sun needed the life force found in blood and hearts to survive** [citation:2]. It was believed that Tonatiuh was locked in a daily battle with the forces of darkness, and without this nourishment, he would fail to rise [citation:6][citation:3].
*   **The Face of the Calendar:** Tonatiuh is most famously depicted at the center of the Aztec Calendar Stone (Sun Stone), his tongue sticking out in the form of a sacrificial obsidian knife, thirsty for blood [citation:2][citation:7]. This image captures his fierce, demanding nature.

---

### Huitzilopochtli: "Hummingbird of the South" (The Warrior's God)
While Tonatiuh represents the cosmic force of the sun, Huitzilopochtli was the more personal, tribal god of the Aztecs (or Mexica). He was their patron, their protector, and their reason for conquest [citation:3].

*   **The Legendary Birth:** According to Aztec myth, Huitzilopochtli was born in an instant. His mother, the earth goddess Coatlicue, was sweeping a temple on Coatepec (Serpent Mountain) when a ball of feathers fell on her, miraculously impregnating her. Her other children, the goddess Coyolxauhqui and the 400 stars (Centzon Huitznahuah), were shamed by this and plotted to kill her. At the moment of their attack, Huitzilopochtli sprang from his mother's womb fully grown, armed with a fire serpent (*Xiuhcoatl*). He dismembered his sister and scattered her body, then chased off his brothers. This story was the sacred justification for Aztec warfare and human sacrifice [citation:8].
*   **A God of Total War:** Huitzilopochtli's divine mission was clear: war without mercy [citation:1]. He was the celestial warrior who reenacted his birth battle every day, leading the sun across the sky and fighting against the moon and stars. For the Aztecs, capturing enemy warriors for sacrifice was not just a political act; it was a direct way to give their patron god strength for this cosmic war [citation:3].
*   **The Hummingbird Symbol:** His name means "Hummingbird of the South" (or "Left"). The Aztecs saw the hummingbird as a fierce, energetic, and swift creature—a perfect symbol for a fallen warrior. They believed that the souls of warriors who died in battle were reborn as hummingbirds, flitting from flower to flower, returning to the sun after death [citation:1].
