## A note from the founder *(Beth: rewrite in your words)*

I spent ten years as a school-bus driver and as an English Language Arts educator up to seventh grade in public education. I know the chaos of eighty children on a bus, and I know how little respect the people raising our children get. I'm building ORION because I've seen what good structure, freedom within limits and a respected adult can do, and I've also seen what games do to children when nobody designs them with care.

**I'm not asking you to endorse AI.** I'm asking you to hold the wheel on the part of this platform that touches children, so that it is built the way you would build it.

## What ORION is, in one paragraph

ORION is a planned online world: a game, a classroom and a project workshop in one. Players learn real skills, such as languages, maths, building and teamwork, through quests and play. What they make can grow into real-world projects. AI helpers ("council members") assist, but **humans always decide**: capability never automatically becomes authority. It is a design, with no live build yet, which is exactly why now is the moment to shape it.

## Why a Montessori educator might care

| Montessori principle | How ORION's locked design already leans that way | Where we need you |
|---|---|---|
| **Freedom within limits** | Players choose their path; nobody is forced to stay in a class that is crushing them. Parents set the limits (a daily cap per platform, locks, custom tracks). | What the limits should be at each age |
| **Prepared environment** | Separate spaces for under-18s; family and classroom "bubbles" that a parent or teacher sets up | Designing those spaces: quiet zones, materials, order |
| **Follow the child** | "Enter where you are, move when you are ready" (the 3-1-3 principle); learning carries into the endgame | Pacing, readiness signs and how to observe without hovering |
| **Self-correction and no shame** | No permanent death; the escape pod is never punished; mistakes are reversible; feedback must not create shame cycles | How feedback should look and feel |
| **Mixed ages, the respected adult** | Classrooms and public boards; mentors redirect, they don't punish | Mixed-age rules for 13–17 |
| **Concrete before abstract** | Real-world play: the plan is to "play life like a game", quest through your real day and keep interacting with the real world | Which skills should stay off-screen |

## Child-safety rules already locked (non-negotiable)

- No adult is ever alone with a child, except the child's verified guardian.
- Under-13s are never with another player unless their guardian is present.
- Educators reach students **only** through classrooms, public boards and a parent portal. There are no private messages.
- Parents get the full transcript if they weren't in the room.
- When a guardian leaves, the child stays safe in a sealed room. Nobody else can enter.
- An upset child is redirected, not kicked out, and the parent is called in.
- One account per human, with identity checks before anyone can contact a child.

The full structure is in `ORION_UNDER18_STRUCTURE.md`, with its open questions.

## What we're asking for

Choose any level that suits you:

1. **Reader** (1–2 hours): read the under-18 structure and tell us what's wrong.
2. **Advisor** (a short call each month): answer the open questions (rules for 13–17, quiet zones, what a parent's "emotional state" view should show).
3. **Co-designer**: design the under-18 spaces and the education track with us. You'd be named as their designer.
4. **Pilot classroom** (later, only when there's a safe build): try a small, supervised session.

**What you get:**
- Your name on what you design (creator credit, which only you can change).
- A veto on child-facing features in your area.
- No obligation to use or promote AI in your own classroom.
- Founders credit as an early collaborator.

**What you will never be asked to do:**
- endorse a product;
- share student data;
- put children into an unfinished build.
