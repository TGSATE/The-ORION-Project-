## 2. Credit that can't be scrubbed: what's actually possible 🛡️

The full page is `ORION_CREDIT_THAT_TRAVELS.md`. Short version:

- **Metadata can always be removed.** That goes for photo data, file properties and code comments. Locking it isn't possible, so it can't be what protects people.
- **These are what make credit hard to erase:**
  1. **A `CONTRIBUTORS.md` file** at the top of your GitHub repo, listing everyone. I've drafted it from the credit ledger (`orion/r21/github_ready/CONTRIBUTORS.md`). **Check the names before you upload it.**
  2. **GitHub's own history.** Every change saved on GitHub records who made it and when, and everyone can see it. Removing someone's name later leaves a visible trail.
  3. **A dated public fingerprint.** Post the contributors file's fingerprint (a short code computed from the file) on X once per round. If anyone later claims the list was different, the dated post shows what it said on that day. The steps are on the page.
  4. **Credits inside the game itself:** a credits screen and a line on the ORION Engine welcome page. A copy that deletes them looks like a copy that deleted them.
- **Your repo currently says "License: None"** (your screenshot, album #1440). Whether anyone is *legally required* to keep the credit list depends on the license and contributor agreements you choose. That is a question for a lawyer, and I can't advise on it. The contributors file still works as a public record either way.
- **Checked in Lean (§6):** in the way the credit list is kept, adding a round's names **never drops anyone** and **never lists anyone twice**.

## 3. The translator between you and the coders 🗣️

The full page is `ORION_TEAM_TRANSLATOR_LOOP.md`. It has three parts.

**A. The UIAR's ask-once ladder.** This is your "until it hits a brick wall" idea, step by step:
1. A teammate uses a word you don't know, or you use one they don't.
2. The UIAR first looks it up in **the team dictionary** and **every earlier round**.
3. If it finds the word, it gives the plain meaning **and says where it was said before**.
4. **Only if nobody has ever explained it**, the UIAR asks the person who used it one clear question that names the options.
5. The answer goes into the dictionary, so **nobody is asked that word again**.

**B. The three-line readback.** It's ready to paste to the team. Each round, every teammate answers three lines in plain words:
1. "What I understood from Beth's last update is…"
2. "What I'm building right now is…" (one sentence, plus a link)
3. "What I need from Beth is…" (a yes/no or pick-one question)

This tells you whether they actually read your update, without you having to chase anyone.

**C. A starter dictionary** of about 30 entries, for example: *"lock it with 💎"* = "freeze the spec"; *"spaghetti"* = "unsorted backlog"; *"Kronos distress"* = "the player-frustration signal"; *"fork"* = "someone's own copy of a project".
