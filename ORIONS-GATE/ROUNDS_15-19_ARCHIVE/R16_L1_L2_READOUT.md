## 4. Your phone camera and the "find my glasses in the dark" quest 📱🕶️

**Your idea, as I understand it:** you wake up in a pitch-dark room and can't find your glasses. You talk to your phone, it starts recording and turns the search into a **quest**: it offers paths (turn on the flashlight, sweep the camera around, and so on), the UIAR lays the quest over what you're actually doing, and your frustration becomes a game. Or, if you'd rather, you "just figure out how to find your shit on your own" 😄.

### Way 1: record both cameras at once

- **iPhone:** I can't see which model you have. On the **iPhone 17 family**, Apple's Camera app has a built-in **Dual Capture** video mode that records the front and back cameras together (check the Video mode options). **Older iPhones** (XS/XR and later) can do it through third-party "dual camera" apps that use Apple's multi-camera feature. Search the App Store for "dual camera video", and check the reviews and the permissions an app asks for. I can't vouch for a particular app.
- **Samsung Galaxy:** many recent models have **Dual recording** or **Director's View** under Camera → More. Other Android phones vary.

### Way 2: one camera (works on any phone)

- **Back camera facing out** at the room. Your **voice is still recorded** by the microphone, so your narration is the quest log. This is the simplest way.
- **Screen recording** (iPhone: Control Center → Screen Recording; long-press it to turn the **microphone on**) captures whatever app is on screen plus your voice. It's the best way to record an "overlay": open an AI that can see through the camera (below) and screen-record the session.

### Hands-free in the dark

- **"Hey Siri, turn on the flashlight."** Then **"Hey Siri, take a video"** opens the Camera ready to record. Depending on your iOS version you may need one tap.
- **Make your own "Start Quest" button** in the **Shortcuts** app: *Set Flashlight: On* → *Speak Text: "Quest started: find your glasses. Path one, light. Path two, scan. Path three, retrace your steps."* → *Open Camera*. Name it "Start Quest" and say **"Hey Siri, Start Quest."** Under **Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap** you can also run it by double-tapping the back of the phone, with no buttons to find in the dark.
- **AI "eyes":** Gemini Live (camera sharing), ChatGPT voice with video and Meta AI can look through your phone camera while you talk. You can ask "do you see my glasses?" as you sweep the room. They can be wrong, so treat the answer as a hint.
- **Smart glasses:** if the glasses you were charging are smart glasses, check their companion app for a last-connected or find feature (I can't confirm yours has one). For ordinary glasses, a small tracker tag on the case solves it for next time.

### As a game quest (proposal 💜): *The Lost Item Quest*

1. **Light path:** turn on the light (always offered first, so nobody trips in the dark).
2. **Scan path:** sweep the camera slowly; the AI marks "warm/cold".
3. **Memory path:** UIAR asks "Where did you last have them? What were you doing?"
4. **Solo path:** "Figure it out on your own." No hints, more XP: the same idea as the hardcore gate.

Finding the item earns XP for your UIAR companion. **Safety and privacy rules to carry into the build:** don't walk while looking at the screen; ask before recording anyone else in the room; keep bedroom video **on the phone** by default (nothing uploaded unless you choose); younger tiers follow the existing age rules. **Today this is a do-it-yourself setup (Shortcuts + camera + an AI with camera vision), not a finished app** that overlays quests automatically.

- **Hourglass inside the torus:** V. Logvinovich, J.-C. Pérez and E. File: `ref_Logvinovich_Perez_Newton_2026_fig2-3_CC-BY-4.0.png`.

**Important before you approach him:** that June paper **already puts the hourglass inside the torus.** It describes the 2D hourglass as a "projection embedded within the 3D IT³" torus. So "hourglass + torus together" is their own published picture. **Your step further would be the sphere.** Show him that specifically, so it reads as building on their figure rather than repeating it.

**The geometry that makes "they make a sphere" exact.** I drew `orion/round16_L1/torus_hourglass_sphere.png` (and a scalable `.svg`): a side cut through a **sphere** (gold), a **horn torus** (blue, a doughnut whose hole has shrunk to a single point) and an **hourglass** (red) whose waist sits at that same point. Three facts behind the picture are **checked in Lean** in `RequestProject/R16/TorusHourglassSphere.lean`:

1. **Shrink a torus's ring radius to zero and you get exactly a sphere.** The equations pick out the same points. In that sense, a sphere is the limit of a torus.
2. **A horn torus with tube radius \(r\) fits exactly inside the sphere of radius \(2r\)**, touching it all the way round the equator.
3. **The hourglass meets the sphere in exactly two circles**, one above and one below the waist, at heights given by \(z^2 = k^2\rho^2/(1+k^2)\).

The torus's pinch point, the hourglass's waist and the sphere's centre are **the same point**. That's a clean visual for your idea. **What this does and doesn't show:** these are facts about shapes in ordinary space. They don't confirm the IT³/Perez physics claims, the critique of the hourglass still stands (J. Nader, Zenodo 22071951), and the Clay problems are still marked open.
