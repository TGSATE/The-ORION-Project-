## 1. Phone as a user-chosen Galaxy Guide

**Problem reported:** in landscape, a document's composing area and keyboard fight for space and may sit on the wrong side for a person using one hand. Turning the screen does not necessarily turn an app's editor. The user should not have to exceed their own safe activity limit to communicate.

**Layout request:**
1. The compose field is visible and small enough to leave the document/context in view. Allow a corner or side position with a resize handle. Keep focus when turning the screen if the app supports it; save a draft before layout changes. Never cover the last lines of dictation with the keyboard.
2. A single toggle selects left, right or bottom dock, regardless of handedness label. Let the user relocate the toggle itself. Do not assume left-handed players want the left dock.
3. Dock essentials: microphone/stop, return/new line, backspace, undo, punctuation, numbers, emoji, send/confirm. Make **send** distinct from **new line** with confirmation for important actions. Full keyboard and compact keys are optional, not forced. Large touch targets, switch focus and labels for screen readers matter more than a decorative rail.
4. Offer dictation, tap, stylus, external keyboard and controller/switch as parallel input routes, not a ladder of worth. Give spoken/dictated text an easy correction step. Let a person use the phone as a controller for a TV *only after a paired, opt-in connection*; keep a direct non-phone controller path.
5. Orientation and input choices persist per user and can be changed mid-session without losing draft text. Keep an explicit “restore default” option. No auto-mirror that reverses words. Test short sessions with people with different hand/dexterity/vision needs; do not demand evidence of disability.

**Acceptance tests to propose, not yet performed:** landscape with the device on a lap and one hand unused; voice composing while the screen rotates; left/right swap without losing text; both keyboard and mic disabled to confirm an accessible route remains; backspace and undo after dictation; separate “send” and “newline”; 200% text scaling; controller and screen reader; accidental activation recovery. A tester can stop at any time. This is not a claim that Android, iOS, Google Docs, a specific keyboard, or ORION already implements these controls.

## 2. Cursive / stylus magic, without a motor-skill gate

**Creative mapping:** choose a block-like shape to set a spell frame; compose a sequence of symbols/strokes; show a preview; confirm the resulting spell. Optional continuous-flow practice can alter *style feedback* or optional mastery badges. Hard-coded character forms should not be taken from a living culture without consent and attribution.

**Equal outcome paths:** stylus handwriting, icon sequence, controller/switch scanning, accessible on-screen keyboard, voice with confirmation. None requires small accurate strokes or continuous hand motion to reach an essential spell, Gauntlet level or reward. Prevent rapid button-mashing with a **timed deliberate confirmation or sequence check** that works with every input path; give settings for timing and repetition, and never mistake involuntary repeated activation for cheating. An unfinished spell should remain editable; if the player confirms a partial spell, show the last completed checkpoint and offer a safe “try again,” not confiscate progress. Whether a partial effect can be cast is Beth's decision.

**Testable game requirements:** (a) every spell reachable by stylus is reachable by switch/controller/voice or icons; (b) suspending a stroke does not erase completed sequences; (c) alternative input does not lower the spell result; (d) the player can inspect exactly which stage completed and why; (e) a timeout can be extended or disabled; (f) consent before invoking any consequential or public action. These are specifications for future implementation, not formal claims about a built engine.
