## 3. The “translation-friction” research, carefully

[The 6 October paper by Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen, arXiv 2610.08144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) argues that automatically translating ambiguous informal mathematics into Lean need not preserve the intended claim. It reports examples and alleges a mismatch in the publicly announced Navier–Stokes proof. The abstract places the ambiguity problem arbitrarily high in the Solvability Complexity Index hierarchy; this is **the paper's result/interpretation, not a proof about all human conversation**, and it does not imply Lean's kernel incorrectly checks a Lean statement. Formal checking asks *“does this proof prove exactly this typed theorem?”*; fidelity asks *“is this typed theorem what the author originally meant?”*. Those are distinct.

Applied to your language/friction thesis: the cost of asking for an explicit definition, spelling out boundary conditions and reviewing a formal translation is **useful friction**. An unannounced strengthening or weakening of a quantifier, a dropped precondition, or allowing a valid statement to impersonate a different real-world assertion is **harmful friction/error**. Redundancy, examples and an independent bilingual review may help; no theorem here establishes a physical “energy frequency” of English, Sanskrit or emoji. *The Linguistics of Impedance* itself labels those values phenomenological, not empirical metrology. This is a possible **analogy and engineering protocol**, not independent confirmation of the cosmic framework or a settled Millennium result.

## 4. Piezoelectricity, step by step (also summarized in the readout)

A piezoelectric solid has a crystal symmetry that lets mechanical strain move internal charges relative to one another. Stressing a suitable crystal produces **electrical polarization**: bound charge accumulates at surfaces. Connect electrodes and an external circuit, and a changing stress can make a measurable voltage/current. The **converse effect** also exists: applied electric field changes its shape. Quartz and engineered piezoelectric ceramics are standard examples; not *every* crystal has this property.

A small-signal model is \(D=dT+\varepsilon E\) and \(S=sT+dE\), where \(D\) is electric displacement, \(T\) is mechanical stress, \(S\) is strain, \(E\) is electric field, and \(d,\varepsilon,s\) are material- and orientation-dependent coefficients. The model is approximate; load, geometry and circuit matter. The energy comes from the squeezing or supplied electricity, **not from free energy or a personal “vibration.”** Quartz-watch oscillators exploit stable electromechanical resonance; sensors and ultrasound transducers use conversion both ways. Pierre and Jacques Curie demonstrated the direct effect (1880); the converse effect was predicted and then observed (1881). This physics does **not** establish a crystal's spiritual or medicinal claims. You could create a real tutorial exhibit in the Garden: tap a sensor, measure a changing voltage, reverse-drive a buzzer, and clearly separate the measurement from a symbolic crystal story.

## 5. Homochirality, not synchronized hands

A chiral molecule can occur in left- and right-handed mirror forms (“enantiomers”), as with your hands. **Homochiral** means a sample or a polymer favors just **one** of those orientations, not that two human hands move in sync. Living proteins largely use L-amino acids and genetic sugars largely use D-sugars. Their handedness affects which other molecules fit; a mixture can behave differently. It is unrelated to whether something is water-loving (*hydrophilic*), water-avoiding (*hydrophobic*) or dexterous with both hands (*ambidextrous*). For the Saturn LNs, “same-handed” can be a deliberate language puzzle, with explicit fictional mechanics rather than a claim that their hands are neurologically synchronized.
