## 8. Research requested in full: “Babel at the mathematics frontier” 🗣️

Here is the substance rather than just a slogan. A mathematical paper has layers: an **informal intended assertion**, a **formal typed statement**, a **machine-checked proof of that statement**, and possibly a **real-world claim**. Proving the third does *not by itself* show the first and second match. A recent [arXiv paper by Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen (2610.08144)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144), titled *Navier-Stokes lost in translation*, argues that translating ambiguous natural-language mathematics into Lean can **silently change meaning**; its authors specifically challenge correspondence between a public Navier–Stokes proof and a formal version. They present ambiguity examples and a result in a computability-complexity hierarchy. **This is the authors' analysis, not my independent proof of their allegations, nor a criticism of Lean's ability to check a precisely stated theorem.**

**A little example:** “every player can reach a safe zone” might mean *one particular safe zone works for all players* (\(\exists z\,\forall p\)) or *each player has their own reachable safe zone* (\(\forall p\,\exists z\)). Both can be precisely proved in a model; proving the easier one does not establish the stronger design promise. Asking which meaning you intended, checking edge cases and recording the exact formalized theorem adds **necessary friction**. Hiding the swapped quantifiers, conflating a model with shipped game code, or claiming the prize is already won is **harmful friction**. That is a disciplined way to explore your “language sets friction” thesis—not empirical confirmation that a human body needs a particular linguistic frequency.

**Homochiral** = “of the same molecular handedness,” as with a set of all left-hand-shaped forms of a molecule; **not** two human hands moving in sync. Life uses predominantly one handedness for its protein amino acids and the other for its DNA/RNA sugars. *Hydrophobic* concerns avoiding water; *hydrophilic* means water-attracting; *ambidextrous* means skilled with both hands. You could give the Saturn LNs a playful “same-handed” metaphor, explicitly fictional rather than misteach the chemistry.

**Piezoelectricity:** squeezing (or stretching) **certain** crystals shifts positive and negative charges, making an **electric polarization**; if electrodes are connected, a changing squeeze can drive measurable current. Reverse the input—apply an electric field—and a piezoelectric element **changes shape**. Quartz resonators help keep time; ceramics in ultrasound probes send/receive vibrations; pressure sensors measure force. The energy comes from mechanical or electrical input, **not free energy** and not proof crystals heal or encode thoughts. Pierre and Jacques Curie demonstrated the direct effect in 1880, and the reverse was demonstrated after Lippmann predicted it in 1881. For a Garden exhibit: measure a sensor's voltage under tapping, then show electrically driven sound, with clearly labelled **science** and **symbolic crystal art**. The equations, units and limitations are in `R15_L8_RESEARCH.md` §4.
