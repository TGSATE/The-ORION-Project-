## 3. Rat Slap (cards): your rules, retyped

*Source: your words on the Round 10 page. Name: Rat Slap is your family name for a widely known card game that goes by several cruder names.*

- **Players:** 2 to 4 per deck. Deal the deck out evenly or as close as possible; **the dealer gets the extra card** if it's odd. For more players, **one more deck per four players** (4 players: 1 deck; 8 players: 2 decks; and so on).
- **Start:** the player to the dealer's left begins.
- **Turns:** each player lays down one card, passing the turn to the next player, until someone lays down a **royal**: **Ace, King, Queen, Jack**.
- **A royal starts a battle round.** The next player has a set number of turns to lay down another royal:
  - **Ace → 1 turn**, **King → 2 turns**, **Queen → 3 turns**, **Jack → 4 turns**.
- **If they don't** lay down a royal in time, the player who laid the royal **takes the whole pile**, and starts the next round.
- **If they do**, the battle becomes theirs, and the next player has to answer *their* royal.
- **Slap:** if two cards of the **same rank** (not suit: numbers or faces) land on top of each other, **whoever slaps the pile first takes it**.
- **Back in:** a player who has lost all their cards **can slap a double to win back a place at the table**.
- **Play goes clockwise** until one person holds every card. That person wins.

Beth's note on purpose: these are "base schematic" games for the Game Master skill. Players learn to build their own games by starting from these two and changing them. Both "keep score" with basic maths and pattern recognition.

*(Already proved in Round 9: in the Rat Slap model, no card is ever created or lost.)*

- **R-2.** Slapping when there's no double: in many tables you pay a card as a penalty. Yes or no?
