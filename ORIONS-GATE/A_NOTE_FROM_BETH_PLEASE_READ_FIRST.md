## The ideas, in plain words

1. **Friction keeps a person together.** The main essay (`FRICTION_EXAMINATION.md`) uses a picture from physics: something sliding needs the right amount of friction. Too little and it flies apart. Too much and it gets stuck. Beth says a person (the "avatar") is the same: we need some resistance to stay whole, but too much crushes us.

2. **Language is the dial.** Words can create good friction, like clear limits, honest questions and stories that help us grow. They can also create bad friction: misunderstandings, walls between people, and the "Tower of Babel" feeling where nobody can hear each other. Her writing on Cuneiform, Voynich and the "Languanauts" all explores how language turns that dial.

3. **Old stories, re-read with hope.** Her blog posts take stories like Babel, Creation and Revelation and read them as stories about people waking up and reconnecting, not about punishment and doom.

4. **Orion, the game.** Beth is turning all of this into a world: characters, lore, a game manual and storylines. The **Monster Deck** and the **dragon hunts** are part of that game. The monsters are made-up characters, ways of drawing fears so players can face them together. **They are not real people**, and the game's own rules say so.

5. **Her job and yours.** Beth builds worlds. She doesn't write code. Turning the ideas into apps and pages is the builders' job. Please don't ask her to fix or understand technical things.
