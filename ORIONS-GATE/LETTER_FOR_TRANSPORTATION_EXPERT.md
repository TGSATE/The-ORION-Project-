Dear ____________,

Thank you for taking a minute to read this. I'm Beth (A. Beth Jones, TGSATE). I'm building an online world called **ORION**: part game, part classroom, part community space. I'm not a coder. I'm the person with the ideas, and I have a small team of volunteers and AI helpers turning them into something real.

**Why I'm writing to you, of all people**

Your work is getting people from one place to another **safely**. You know about:

- getting people on board and making sure everyone who got on gets off,
- keeping track of who is where at all times,
- handing people over from one driver, route or stop to the next without anyone falling through the gap,
- extra care for kids and for people who need help getting around,
- what to do when something goes wrong in the middle of a trip.

That is exactly the problem at the centre of what we're building, and nobody on my team has your kind of experience with it.

**What "porting" means for us, in plain words**

In ORION, people move between different spaces:

- from the big public area into a **small private room** (we call these "singularity rooms"),
- from one group into another, or into a brand-new group that grows from a few people,
- and each person can bring an **AI helper** with them (we call the whole setup "UIAR"), which travels with them like a guide.

We call that move **"porting"**. It's like a bus trip, just online. Someone leaves one place, travels, and arrives at another, and the group and the guide have to arrive with them, in the right place, safely.

**Why this matters so much**

Our coders are good at the buttons: making the move *happen*. But what I need help with is the **safety process** around the move, and that's the backbone of the whole thing. If people can't move back and forth safely and reliably, nothing else we build will stand up.

Here are the kinds of questions I'm hoping you can help us answer. You may know these answers from real life far better than any of us:

1. **Boarding and the passenger list.** Before someone moves, what do we check? How do we know everyone who left one room arrived in the next?
2. **Handoffs.** When a person moves from one group to another, who is responsible for them *during* the move, and when does responsibility pass to the next group?
3. **Kids and vulnerable riders.** What extra rules should apply to young people, for example never leaving them alone in a room with an adult they don't know?
4. **Capacity.** How many people should a room or group hold before it splits into a new one?
5. **Emergency stop.** If someone is upset, in danger or being harassed mid-move, how do they get out right away and back to somewhere safe?
6. **Lost passengers.** If a move goes wrong and someone ends up in the wrong place, how do we find them and get them home?
7. **The driver's rules.** What should the AI guide be allowed to do on its own, and what must always be a human's decision?

**What I'm asking for**

Just a conversation to start, whatever you're comfortable with. I'd love to hear how you would set up a safe trip, and we'll translate that into rules for the online world. Your experience and your credit would be recorded in our project, and nothing would be shared publicly without your OK.

**Where things stand, honestly.** ORION is still in the design stage. The rules are written down and some have been checked with a maths tool, but there's no finished app yet. That's why this is the right moment: we want the safety process designed *before* the buttons get built, not patched in afterwards.

Thank you for considering it. Even one conversation would help us more than you might think.

With gratitude,

**Beth**
