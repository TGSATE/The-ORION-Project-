## Spotify: invite collaborators

1. Open the playlist in the Spotify app.
2. Tap the **add-person icon** (a little person with a +) under the playlist title, or tap **⋮ / •••** and choose **Invite collaborators**.
3. Spotify makes an **invite link**. Send it to the person (text, DM, email).
4. When they open it, they can **add, remove and reorder** songs. Free accounts can collaborate.
5. To stop it: **⋮ / •••** → remove a collaborator, or make the playlist non-collaborative again.

**Good to know:** collaborators can also delete songs. For the master playlist, you might keep it yours and make *separate* collaborative ones (see the three buses, T06).

## YouTube: invite collaborators

1. Open the playlist in the YouTube app (it must be **public or unlisted**, not private).
2. Tap the **pencil / edit** icon, or **⋮**, and look for **Collaborate**.
3. Turn on **Collaborators can add videos to this playlist**.
4. Copy the **invite link** and send it.
5. Collaborators can add videos and remove the ones they added. You can remove anyone, and you can turn off new invites.

**Good to know:** playlists marked "made for kids" can't have collaborators.

## "Linking it all together"

- **Spotify and YouTube don't sync with each other automatically.** Each playlist is updated on its own.
- There are third-party services that copy a playlist from one to the other (TuneMyMusic and Soundiiz are two well-known ones). They need you to sign in to both accounts, so read what access they ask for before using one. I haven't used them on your account.
- The simplest system: **Spotify is the master.** Once a month, add whatever's new to YouTube (or the other way round, if YouTube is the one people use more).
